# Meta-Harness: claim-by-claim audit

Paper: Lee, Nair, Zhang, Lee, Khattab, Finn, "Meta-Harness: End-to-End Optimization of Model Harnesses", arXiv 2603.28052v1. It was read through third-party mirrors: `scratchpad/papers/meta-harness.txt` (HTML clipping) and `meta-harness-pdftext.txt` (PDF text, the source of Table 2's numbers).
Implementation audited: `rsi/metaharness/`, `rsi/domains/memoclassify/`, at commit `75c8464`.
Evidence audited:
- `results/metaharness-solpi/m1..m5*.json`, `live_smoke.json` and `live_reanalysis.json`;
- `validation/metaharness-solpi/{RUNS.md, AUDIT.md, mh_*}`;
- the spec in `docs/methods/metaharness-solpi/paper-spec.md` Part A and the implementation notes in `docs/methods/metaharness-solpi/implementation.md`.

The audit itself changed no code. Four offline checks were run with scratch scripts outside the repo, in `scratchpad/claims_mh/c1..c4_*.py`. They made no LLM calls and used less than 3 minutes of CPU in total. Their results are quoted inline as **[c1]–[c4]**.

**Update after the fixes (2026-09-25).** Every mismatch the audit found (N1–N11) and the two validation items still open (register #10, #24) were then fixed, documented or given the right disposition; one more bug turned up while fixing (N12). The "Fix log" section at the end lists each one with its fix, file, regression test and evidence. M1–M5 were re-run at full settings, a new M6 measures the winner's curse, the stale live numbers were regenerated (a $0 cache replay plus a fresh live run), and the paper's proposer type (a coding agent) ran live for the first time (haiku, 2 iterations). The claim rows below give the verdict **after** the fixes; where it changed, the row says "was …". Live spend for the fixes: $0.98.

**Retry round 2 (2026-09-29).** Five verdicts changed: M9 and K4 PARTIAL → REPRODUCED, L2 NOT REPRODUCED → PARTIAL, L4 CONTRADICTED → PARTIAL, and **Q4 PARTIAL → NOT REPRODUCED** (a downgrade: against faithful OpenEvolve / TTT-Discover / GEPA selection rules at the paper's budget, the mock Meta-Harness no longer wins). Q5 stays NOT REPRODUCED with stronger evidence. The first live LLM test of the history views (M9, haiku) found a small, significant gain from traces over scores only (+1.2 points) and no gain from fair LLM summaries. See "Retry round 2" at the end (preregistration, per-claim table, runs). Live spend $3.85.

Changed verdicts: M2 (traces) and M7 (skill) PARTIAL → REPRODUCED. No verdict got worse. The quantitative verdicts did not move: M1–M4 re-ran to identical selection numbers, because the fixes change what is logged and shown to a proposer, not the offline mock's choices.

**What the numbers show.**
- **The loop is faithful.** The outer-loop mechanics are sound and pass an independent audit: evaluate every valid candidate, no parent selection, a Pareto frontier over (accuracy, context), and one sealed test evaluation at finalisation. Since the fixes, exact ties keep registration order, as in the release, and the context objective counts every model call of a query, so a tiny last call can no longer hide the context (N2).
- **Every quantitative headline stays unreproduced.** None of the paper's headline numbers can be reproduced here: +7.7 over ACE with 4× fewer context tokens, +4.7 on math across five models, the TerminalBench-2 numbers, the Table 3 ablation magnitudes, and 82 files read per iteration.
  - Three of these domains need infrastructure and models we do not run: GPT-OSS-120B/20B, a 535K-problem math corpus, and Harbor with Opus 4.6 at about $500 per TB2 iteration.
  - The CPU analogue (MemoClassify, MemoLM, MockProposer) reproduces only their *direction*.
- **The proposer is still not the paper's.** The paper's central component is an Opus-scale coding agent that decides for itself what to read. It still has not been run at that scale.
  - What changed: the `AgentProposer` now has the release's prototyping (Bash allow-listed to `python3` and read-only commands), can write post-eval reports, and logs the files it actually opens. One small live run with haiku as the coding agent (2 iterations, $0.74) opened 8 of 20 and 8 of 40 history files, ran Python 12 times per iteration to analyse traces and prototype, and produced three harnesses that match `fewshot_all`'s search accuracy at 3.5–4.8× less context.
  - The one-completion `RewriteProposer` now always renders trace excerpts (it had shown 0 of 42 traces on MemoClassify, N3).
  - The offline evidence for "full history wins" still comes from a hand-written mock whose trace diagnosis is richer than its summary diagnosis *by construction*, and which reads only its parents' traces (N4, now documented and reflected in its read accounting).
- **The overfitting caveats reproduce.** RRSI found that Meta-Harness transfers under 1 point, and the overview says there is no guard against test-specific edits. Here, leakage reaches the frontier when it is injected (M5); since N12 a table lookup costs no context, so every leaky candidate sits on the frontier. A live haiku run put a template-regex solver on the frontier. Offline, single-seed selection has a small winner's curse (M6: +0.9 points on the selected harness; 9% of incumbent changes are false) [c2].
  - The RRSI magnitude does not reproduce: without leakage, the simulated transfer gain is +10 points, not under 1.

### What the round shows

- **The Meta-Harness advantage over structured optimisers does not survive faithful baselines on the CPU analogue.** With the paper's budget and real selection rules, GEPA's Pareto-sampled reflection and TTT-Discover's PUCT reuse match the mock Meta-Harness (Q4 downgraded, Q5 more clearly negative). The mock proposer only uses its parents' traces, so this analogue cannot show a benefit from reading the *whole* history; the paper's claim needs a live LLM proposer in every arm (about 20 proposer calls per run, times 5 arms and several seeds).
- **With a live LLM proposer, traces help a little.** In the first live test of the history views, haiku's proposals were 1.2 points better with traces than with scores only (all 5 snapshots agree), and fair LLM summaries added nothing over scores. The direction matches the paper; the size (about a tenth of Table 3's gap) does not.
- **Hard-coding depends on the benchmark.** On MemoClassify no live proposal hard-coded anything (0/50). On AgentQA, whose questions come from four templates, a third of the proposals keyed on those templates and one of them was selected as best.
